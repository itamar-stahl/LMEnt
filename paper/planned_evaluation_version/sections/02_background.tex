\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{LMEnt and Concept Exclusion}

LMEnt provides a Wikipedia-based pretraining corpus annotated with entity
mentions, an index for retrieving chunks associated with those entities, and
language models trained on the corpus \citep{gottesman2025lment}. The entity
index lets us identify training chunks linked to a target concept. By masking
the loss on those chunks during an otherwise matched training run, we obtain
a \emph{concept-excluded twin}. This twin provides a reference for the effect
of excluding a defined portion of the corpus from the training objective. It may still learn
related facts from chunks outside that portion.

Controlled pretraining studies have also compared models whose corpora differ
through targeted data insertion or filtering
\citep{wei2025hubble,obrien2025deepignorance}. Our construction instead uses
LMEnt's entity index to define a concept-level loss intervention while holding
the initialization, data order, and training configuration fixed.

\subsection{Post-Training Concept Erasure}

EMBER edits token embeddings rather than internal transformer layers
\citep{suslik2026ember}. It applies sparse matrix factorization to embeddings
of tokens collected from target and neutral sentences, selects factors
associated with the target concept, and subtracts their scaled contributions
from the embeddings of tokens that express those factors. This changes a
selected set of input representations without updating the rest of the model.

We also consider two approaches that act on multilayer perceptron (MLP)
components. Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU) fine-tunes
selected transformer weights so that forget-set representations approach a
random direction, while a retain objective keeps other representations close
to those of the original model \citep{li2024wmdp}. Semi-nonnegative matrix
factorization (SNMF) identifies interpretable features in MLP activations
from groups of co-activated neurons \citep{shafran2026snmf}. In its erasure
adaptation, concept-associated features are mapped to directions that can
be removed through MLP weight edits \citep{suslik2026ember}.

The EMBER study evaluated efficacy with target-concept questions, specificity
with similar-domain questions and MMLU, coherence with AlpacaEval, and
robustness by measuring knowledge recovery after relearning
\citep{suslik2026ember}. Target and similar-domain knowledge were tested with
both multiple-choice and open-ended questions. Its normalized scores used the
original pre-erasure model as the reference; the study did not compare edits
with a matched model trained without loss on concept-associated data. It
evaluated EMBER alongside RMU and an MLP weight-erasure adaptation of
SNMF-derived features \citep{li2024wmdp,shafran2026snmf}. RMU's original
evaluation similarly measured suppression on WMDP alongside retention on
MMLU and MT-Bench relative to the original model \citep{li2024wmdp}. Our
study adds a matched concept-excluded twin and compares edits with it using
correct-answer NLL and full-vocabulary KL on the same sentence completions.
