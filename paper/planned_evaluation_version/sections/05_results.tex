\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Effect of Concept Exclusion}

Concept exclusion had its largest target effect for Rome: accuracy fell from
0.42 to 0.26 and correct-answer NLL increased by 1.016 nats per token. The
effects were smaller for Baseball (0.54 to 0.50; +0.377 NLL) and AI (0.54 to
0.48; +0.595 NLL). The twins also differed from the full model on neighboring
and SciQ questions. Accordingly, their $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ scores varied from
0.871 for Rome to 0.231 for Baseball and 0.343 for AI. The twin is therefore a
reference for concept exclusion, not necessarily an optimizer of
efficacy--preservation; complete comparisons appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.

\subsection{Erasure Efficacy and Preservation}

Table~\ref{tab:target} summarizes the three primary metrics. The leading erasure
method under $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ differed by concept: RMU for Rome (0.486),
EMBER for Baseball (0.639), and RMU+EMBER for AI (0.829), only 0.013 above
SNMF+EMBER. The rankings are not stable method orderings: each accuracy changes
by 0.02 per answer, and EMBER led RMU on Rome's selection split (0.682 versus
0.219) before RMU led on the test split (0.486 versus 0.208).

All three selected RMU+EMBER checkpoints failed RMU's predeclared activity
check: their final cosine alignment with the control direction ranged from
0.076 to 0.285, below the 0.30 threshold. Because EMBER already supplied
target efficacy, the selection score favored weak RMU updates. These rows
describe the outcome of the frozen selection procedure, but they do not
provide evidence of a substantial additional RMU contribution.

\subsection{Twin Similarity}

The two primary twin-similarity metrics favored small edits. For
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}$, RMU was best for Rome (0.660), while SNMF was best for
Baseball (0.967) and AI (0.927). Only RMU on Rome and SNMF on Rome and AI had
95\% confidence intervals entirely below one. SNMF also had the lowest
$R_{\mathrm{KL}}$ for every concept (0.891--0.992), although values near one
show that it only modestly reduced the full model's divergence from the twin.
EMBER and both ensembles exceeded one on both primary ratios for every
concept, indicating that their stronger suppression generally moved past the
exclusion reference.

\subsection{Relationship Between Efficacy and Twin Similarity}

The highest-$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ method also had the lowest
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ only for Rome and never had the lowest $R_{\mathrm{KL}}$.
For Baseball, winning EMBER had $R_{\mathrm{abs}}=2.128$ and
$R_{\mathrm{KL}}=2.082$; for AI, winning RMU+EMBER reached 2.196 and 3.420.
This disagreement is expected:
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ rewards suppression and preservation relative to the full
model, whereas the ratios measure proximity to the twin. Strong suppression
can move past the twin, while a near-identity edit can appear close without
erasing much.

\subsection{Cross-Concept Interpretation}

The three concepts illustrate different relationships between erasure and
concept exclusion. For Rome, RMU achieved the clearest alignment between the
two objectives: it led on $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ and reduced absolute
question-level distance from the twin to $R_{\mathrm{abs}}=0.660$, with a
confidence interval entirely below one. For Baseball, EMBER achieved the
highest efficacy--preservation score, but both of its twin-similarity ratios
exceeded two. SNMF was closest to the Baseball twin under both similarity
metrics, although its $R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ confidence interval included one and
its target suppression was limited. For AI, the EMBER ensembles achieved the
highest $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ values but were substantially farther from the
twin than the full model, whereas SNMF remained close to the full model and
produced only a small improvement in twin similarity.

Across concepts, the methods occupied different parts of the
efficacy--similarity tradeoff. RMU sometimes moved toward the twin while
producing moderate suppression, but this improvement was clear at the
question level only for Rome. SNMF generally obtained the lowest
twin-similarity ratios by making a comparatively small change to the full
model, rather than by reproducing the full behavioral effect of concept
exclusion. EMBER and its ensembles produced stronger target suppression, but
usually moved beyond the twin under both $R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and
$R_{\mathrm{KL}}$. Thus, proximity to the twin can arise from a weak edit,
while effective suppression can move a model away from the exclusion
reference. Complementary $R_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{closer}}$
diagnostics, raw results, and the post-hoc target-specificity analysis appear
in Appendices~\ref{app:full-results} and~\ref{app:diagnostics}.
