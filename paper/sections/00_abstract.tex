Concept-erasure methods are commonly evaluated by how much they reduce target
knowledge relative to the original model. This comparison cannot determine
whether an erased model resembles one that never learned the concept. We study
this question with matched 1B-parameter language models trained on the same
entity-annotated Wikipedia corpus, with either Ancient Rome or Baseball chunks
masked from the training loss. We compare these concept-excluded references
with EMBER, an embedding-editing erasure method, and evaluate similarity along
behavioral, distributional, parameter-space, and specificity dimensions.
EMBER does not reproduce a single, consistent approximation to concept
exclusion. For Ancient Rome it suppresses target text substantially more than
exclusion and also harms control text; for Baseball it undershoots exclusion on
held-out text, while the small question-side exclusion effect cannot be
separated from ordinary between-twin variation. In the completed
Baseball weight comparison, the EMBER update is nearly orthogonal to the
control-to-excluded displacement. \placeholder{Add the final RMU and SNMF
comparison in one sentence.} Our results motivate evaluating concept
erasure against controlled training baselines with several complementary
instruments rather than treating any single behavioral score as evidence of
never learning.
