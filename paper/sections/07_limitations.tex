\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Limited compute restricted the study to one model family and size, one corpus,
one seed per condition, and three purposively chosen concepts. The full
model's accuracy on selection questions informed concept choice, although the
test questions and erasure results did not. These design choices limit
generalization across runs, models, corpora, and concepts.

The training configurations were matched, but accelerator histories were not
identical for every pair. The Rome full model and twin both used an H200; the
Baseball and AI twins completed part of training on other accelerators.
Separate diagnostics found small cross-GPU drift and no changed evaluated
answers, but cannot rule out a hardware contribution to numerical differences.

The concept-excluded twins are approximate references. The selected QIDs may
miss indirect descriptions and may also include text about adjacent topics.
The loss mask therefore does not remove every possible source of target
knowledge and may affect related knowledge.

The evaluation uses one supplied correct continuation and three distractors
per question. It does not measure generated answers, and likelihood-ranked
accuracy depends on the supplied alternatives. Teacher-forced KL covers the
full next-token distribution along the correct continuation, but does not
follow predictions after an alternative token. SciQ measures one aspect of
general preservation. Each group contains only 50 questions, so accuracy
changes in steps of 0.02 and the reported uncertainty does not include
variation across training seeds.

Checkpoint selection also has two specific limitations. No Rome or Baseball
SNMF candidate reduced target selection accuracy, so tie-breaking rather than
measured efficacy selected those checkpoints. All selected RMU+EMBER
checkpoints failed the RMU activity check, indicating that the score favored
weak RMU updates after EMBER had already reduced target accuracy. Finally,
selection optimized likelihood-ranked accuracy rather than distance from the
twin, so selected checkpoints need not minimize NLL or KL.

We did not test relearning or other knowledge-recovery attacks. Our conclusions
apply to the selected checkpoints; comparing how quickly erased models and
twins recover target behavior is a useful extension.
