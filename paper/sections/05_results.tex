\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Validity of the Never-Trained Baselines}

All three exclusion interventions produced a selective NLL increase.
For Ancient Rome, excluding concept chunks increases the held-out-versus-control
gap by 0.2349 nats/token. Baseball yields an even larger 0.4933 nats/token.
The corresponding paired effects on held-out chunks are $d_z=1.052$ and
$d_z=2.070$; these are not standardized effects for the difference-in-differences
itself. In both arms the per-chunk increase is nearly uniform,
while drift on length-matched general chunks is small (0.0035 and 0.0058
nats/token, respectively). Thus the excluded twins establish a measurable
text-NLL target before any erasure method is judged.

The AI twin masks 19,818 chunks; on its own matched text sample the held-out
loss rises by 0.1561 nats/token versus 0.0017 on control chunks. Its
held-out-minus-control difference-in-differences is 0.1544. These values
validate the AI exclusion intervention, but no EMBER text-loss comparison on
that identical sample is reported yet.

The available general-task check is limited to SciQ. With accuracy based on
character-normalized option scores, the Rome control and excluded twin both
score 0.717; the Baseball
control and excluded twin score 0.717 and 0.697 on 1,000 items, a difference
of approximately 1.4 standard errors. This does not establish preservation
across a broader capability suite or for every erased model.

\subsection{Behavioral Similarity}

On held-out Ancient Rome questions, the excluded model's gold-option
PMI-per-character score falls by 0.2797 relative to control, whereas EMBER falls
by 0.6237. The residual of $-0.3439$ indicates that EMBER suppresses these answers more than
concept exclusion. The neighboring-history set is approximately flat for the
excluded twin. EMBER moves it relative to control on both halves, although the
held-out erased-minus-excluded residual ($-0.1318$) is uncertain (paired
$t$-test $p=0.057$).

Baseball differs. On its target questions the excluded-minus-control change is
small
($-0.0452$ on the held-out half), while EMBER produces a clear larger decline
($-0.1935$). However, a model trained with Rome chunks excluded, but all
Baseball chunks included, reproduces most of the apparent exclusion change
($-0.0355$ on the same split). Scoring the same control and no-Baseball model on
questions about eight untouched concepts also places the Baseball change
within the variation seen on those question sets. The unusual change on
neighboring-sports questions is similarly
reproduced by the no-Rome model.
Consequently, the Baseball question results establish an EMBER effect but do
not establish a behavioral never-training target or an overshoot ratio.

Accuracy obscures much of this behavior. In a separate conditional-likelihood
diagnostic, the excluded Baseball twin's gold score falls relative to control
on 42 of 50 selection questions, including questions both models answer
correctly, while four-option accuracy changes by only three net questions. For
Ancient Rome, EMBER's selected delta reduces accuracy primarily on the split
used to choose it, but the continuous effect generalizes to the held-out split.
An answer-length check found no significant correlation between gold-answer
length and the paired score change in 11 of 12 tested cells; this check does
not rule out other prompt or answer-choice biases.

\paragraph{Artificial intelligence.}
On the common held-out question split, the excluded AI twin changes gold-option
PMI-per-character by $-0.102$ relative to control. EMBER changes it by
$-0.090$, leaving a residual of about $+0.012$; its standardized paired
residual is $d_z=+0.04$ with bootstrap 95\% interval $[-0.23,+0.36]$. The
neighboring-domain changes are likewise similar ($-0.032$ for exclusion and
$-0.033$ for EMBER). This is agreement on this instrument, not a demonstrated
equivalence of models or of their text distributions.

The same common harness gives SNMF $+0.049$ on AI target questions, the wrong
direction for erasure. RMU with retain weight $\alpha=100$ gives $-0.063$;
with $\alpha=10$ it gives $-0.211$, overshooting while also moving the
neighboring set by $-0.131$ rather than the twin's $-0.032$. The $\alpha=10$
target residual has $d_z=-0.41$ with bootstrap interval $[-0.66,-0.16]$.
All four method checkpoints were rescored together; earlier MLP-grid accuracy
numbers use a different question scale and are not pooled with these values.

\subsection{Text-Likelihood Similarity}

Held-out text gives a different picture from the questions. For Ancient
Rome, EMBER's difference-in-differences is 1.3461 nats/token, 5.73 times the
excluded effect. Its median per-chunk shift is nevertheless close to that of
exclusion; the excess is concentrated in a heavy tail. EMBER makes 16.2\% of
Rome chunks more than 3 nats/token worse, whereas the excluded model never
enters that range. General control text also moves by 0.0332 nats/token, compared
with 0.0035 for exclusion.

For Baseball, EMBER instead reaches 0.3368 nats/token against the excluded
effect of 0.4933, or 68\% of the target. Its residual is therefore negative:
the erased model remains better at held-out Baseball text than the excluded
twin. Unlike Ancient Rome, collateral change relative to the excluded model on
general text is not significant (sign-flip $p=0.072$). Hence the selected EMBER
settings overshoot the Rome text baseline but undershoot the Baseball baseline.

\paragraph{Strength and lexical sensitivity.}
A fixed-strength Rome sweep shows that delta 50 nearly matches the exclusion
mean (DiD $+0.2635$ versus $+0.2349$) but yields less than half its median
per-chunk increase ($+0.083$ versus $+0.172$). At delta 200 the median is
closer ($+0.215$), but the mean reaches $+1.3461$ and 16.2\% of chunks exceed
3 nats/token of added loss, versus none under exclusion. Thus changing one
strength does not match both moments on the tested grid. At a matched delta
10, Rome and Baseball reach 0.74 and 0.68 times their respective exclusion
DiD effects; at delta 200, they reach 5.73 and 1.27 times. Concept and strength
interact rather than contributing independent constant factors.

The mismatch has a token-level signature. Among 11 Rome held-out chunks with
none of the 76 edited token IDs, EMBER's loss change is zero while exclusion
still adds $0.1348$ nats/token. For the 1,382 chunks with at least eight edited
token occurrences, EMBER adds $2.4716$ and exclusion $0.2860$. Baseball also
shows zero erasure change on chunks without edited IDs, while its exclusion
effect remains positive. These are small or unevenly sized bins, but they
distinguish localized embedding edits from distributed training effects. At
delta 200, Baseball has more chunks with at least eight edited-token
occurrences than Rome (1,844 versus 1,382), yet its loss increase in that bin
is smaller ($+0.8571$ versus $+2.4716$). Token frequency alone therefore does
not explain the stronger Rome tail.

A cross-concept question probe provides a direct example of lexical spillover.
When the Rome-erased model was scored on 200 Baseball and neighboring-sport
questions, three moved markedly; each contained the edited token
\texttt{distance}. The remaining 197 were effectively unchanged. This probe
locates a concrete source of collateral damage, although three questions do
not measure its overall prevalence.

\subsection{Parameter-Space Similarity}

For Baseball, the tracked aggregate summary reports that the global cosine
between EMBER's update and the control-to-excluded displacement is 0.0010.
Progress along the target direction
is $2.64\times10^{-5}$ and normalized residual distance is 1.0003. Restricting
the calculation to the embedding matrix raises cosine only to 0.0043. Thus the
observed behavioral and text-loss effects do not correspond to meaningful
movement toward the excluded model in parameter space.

The repository does not report an aligned Ancient Rome parameter comparison or
whole-model RMU/SNMF comparisons. The Baseball result therefore describes one
completed parameter-space case.
