\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{LMEnt and Concept Exclusion}

LMEnt provides a Wikipedia-based pretraining corpus annotated with entity
mentions, an index for retrieving chunks associated with those entities, and
language models trained on the corpus \citep{gottesman2025lment}. The entity
index lets us identify training chunks linked to a target concept. By masking
the loss on those chunks during an otherwise matched training run, we obtain
a \emph{concept-excluded twin}. This twin provides a reference for the effect
of excluding a defined portion of the training data. It may still learn
related facts from chunks outside that portion.

\subsection{Post-Training Concept Erasure}

EMBER edits token embeddings rather than internal transformer layers
\citep{suslik2026ember}. It factorizes the embedding matrix into sparse
features, selects features associated with the target concept, and subtracts
their contributions from the embeddings of selected tokens. This makes it
possible to change a small set of input representations without updating the
rest of the model.

We also consider two approaches that act on multilayer perceptron (MLP)
components. Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU) updates selected
MLP parameters to redirect representations of forget-set inputs, while a
retain objective encourages representations of other inputs to remain close
to the original model \citep{li2024wmdp}. Semi-nonnegative matrix
factorization (SNMF) identifies interpretable features in MLP activations
from groups of co-activated neurons \citep{shafran2026snmf}. In its erasure
adaptation, concept-associated features are mapped to directions that can
be removed through MLP weight edits \citep{suslik2026ember}.

The EMBER study evaluated erasure using multiple-choice and open-ended
questions about target concepts, with related-topic questions and general
benchmarks used to assess preservation \citep{suslik2026ember}. It also
examined generation quality and whether target knowledge returned after
relearning. These evaluations compared edited models with the original
model, which had been trained on the target concept. They included EMBER,
RMU, and an MLP weight-erasure adaptation of SNMF-derived features
\citep{li2024wmdp,shafran2026snmf}. RMU's original evaluation likewise
measured target-question performance and general capabilities relative to
the original model. Our study adds a matched concept-excluded twin as a
reference and compares edited models with it using correct-answer NLL and
full-vocabulary KL on the same sentence completions.
