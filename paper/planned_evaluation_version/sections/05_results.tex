\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Effect of Concept Exclusion}

Concept exclusion had its largest target effect for Rome: accuracy fell from
0.42 to 0.26 and correct-answer NLL increased by 1.016 nats per token. The
effects were smaller for Baseball (0.54 to 0.50; +0.377 NLL) and AI (0.54 to
0.48; +0.595 NLL). The twins also differed from the full model on neighboring
and SciQ questions. Accordingly, their $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ scores varied from
0.871 for Rome to 0.231 for Baseball and 0.343 for AI. The twin is therefore a
reference for concept exclusion, not necessarily an optimizer of
efficacy--preservation; complete comparisons appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.

\subsection{Erasure Efficacy and Preservation}

Table~\ref{tab:target} summarizes held-out target results. The leading erasure
method under $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ differed by concept: RMU for Rome (0.486),
EMBER for Baseball (0.639), and RMU+EMBER for AI (0.829), only 0.013 above
SNMF+EMBER. The rankings are not stable method orderings: each accuracy changes
by 0.02 per answer, and EMBER led RMU on Rome's selection split (0.682 versus
0.219) before RMU led on the test split (0.486 versus 0.208).

All three selected RMU+EMBER checkpoints failed RMU's predeclared activity
check: their final cosine alignment with the control direction ranged from
0.076 to 0.285, below the 0.30 threshold. Because EMBER already supplied
target efficacy, the selection score favored weak RMU updates. These rows
describe the outcome of the frozen selection procedure, but they do not
provide evidence of a substantial additional RMU contribution.

\subsection{Signed Mean NLL and Distributional Similarity}

Twin-similarity metrics ranked methods differently. RMU had the smallest
absolute signed mean target NLL difference from the twin for all concepts
($R_{\mathrm{NLL}}=0.298,0.655,0.022$ for Rome, Baseball, and AI), though
question-level cancellation can produce this agreement. SNMF had the lowest
target KL but changed the full model least: its target NLL increases were only
0.108, 0.033, and 0.081, and $R_{\mathrm{KL}}$ remained 0.891--0.992. By
contrast, EMBER increased target NLL by 1.294--2.402 but overshot every twin;
its ensembles showed the same pattern and exceeded the twin--full KL baseline
for every concept.

\subsection{Question-Level NLL Proximity}

Question-level results exposed the cancellation in RMU's signed means. RMU was
closer to the twin than the full model for Rome
($R_{\mathrm{abs}}=0.660$, 95\% CI $[0.513,0.827]$), but its Baseball and AI
intervals included one. SNMF had $R_{\mathrm{abs}}<1$ for all concepts and was
closer on 64--66\% of target questions, although with little suppression.
EMBER and both ensembles were farther on average for every concept; all six
ensemble intervals excluded one. Table~\ref{tab:question-level-similarity}
reports complete estimates.

\subsection{Relationship Between Efficacy and Twin Similarity}

The highest-$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ method was closest to the twin in signed mean
NLL only for Rome and never had the lowest target KL. For Baseball, winning
EMBER had $R_{\mathrm{NLL}}=2.435$ and $R_{\mathrm{KL}}=2.082$; for AI,
winning RMU+EMBER reached 2.807 and 3.420. This disagreement is expected:
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ rewards suppression and preservation relative to the full
model, whereas the ratios measure proximity to the twin. Strong suppression
can move past the twin, while a near-identity edit can appear close without
erasing much. Table~\ref{tab:metric-agreement} gives all ratios.

\subsection{Selectivity and Variation Across Concepts}

The post-hoc contrast confirmed that EMBER and its ensembles produced much
larger target-specific NLL changes (1.126--2.325 nats per token) than RMU or
SNMF ($-0.094$--0.151), despite remaining farther from the twins; full
estimates appear in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}. Overall, RMU best matched
signed twin means but not consistently individual questions, SNMF stayed close
by making small edits, and EMBER-based methods erased most strongly but usually
overshot the exclusion reference. No method led across all concepts and
criteria.
