Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress target knowledge while preserving other behavior, but comparing an erased model to its original counterpart does not reveal whether the erasure truly mimics training without the concept. We evaluate a suite of erasure methods (including EMBER, RMU, and SNMF) across three different concepts, comparing them against both a full model and matched, concept-excluded "twin" models. Our primary metrics go beyond standard efficacy and preservation to measure the probabilistic proximity of the erased models to their twins. We find that the methods that best balance suppression and preservation are generally not those that most closely resemble the twin. Mild edits often fail to achieve sufficient target suppression, whereas more aggressive edits successfully suppress knowledge but drive the model's behavior further away from the twin baseline. Thus, effective target suppression - even when it preserves neighboring and general performance - does not necessarily reproduce the true outcome of concept exclusion.
