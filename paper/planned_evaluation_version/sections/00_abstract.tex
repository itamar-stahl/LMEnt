Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress knowledge of a target concept
while preserving model behavior elsewhere. Standard evaluations compare
erased and original models, but do not show whether erasure reproduces
training without the concept-associated data. We
evaluate this distinction using matched concept-excluded twins for Ancient
Rome, Baseball, and artificial intelligence. For each concept, we compare
EMBER, RMU, and an SNMF-based erasure method with both the full model and its
twin. Evaluation covers target, neighboring-topic, and SciQ questions using
likelihood-ranked accuracy, correct-answer negative log-likelihood, and
teacher-forced full-vocabulary KL divergence. No method dominates across all
criteria. RMU most closely matches the twins' mean target-answer NLL, while
SNMF yields the lowest target KL. EMBER produces the strongest
target-specific NLL changes and the highest combined efficacy--preservation
score for Baseball and AI; RMU achieves the highest score for Rome.
strong target suppression with preserved neighboring and SciQ accuracy does
not imply similarity to the concept-excluded twin: the highest-scoring method
is closest in mean target NLL for only one concept and never has the lowest
target KL. These results show that efficacy--preservation and similarity to a
matched exclusion reference capture distinct properties of concept erasure.
