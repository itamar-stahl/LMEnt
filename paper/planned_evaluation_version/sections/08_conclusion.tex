\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated whether post-training concept erasure reproduces the behavior of
matched models trained without loss on concept-associated chunks. The answer
depends on which property is measured. RMU was closest to the twins in signed
mean target-answer NLL for all three concepts, but the question-level analysis
showed that cancellation explained the apparent Baseball and AI matches. SNMF
changed the full model least and had the lowest target KL, but improved only
slightly over the full model and produced little target erasure. EMBER produced
the strongest target-specific NLL increase among the standalone methods and
the highest combined efficacy--preservation score for Baseball; RMU obtained
the highest score for Rome and RMU+EMBER for AI. Neither RMU+EMBER nor
SNMF+EMBER moved closer to the twin under question-level target NLL distance;
all six ensemble ratios exceeded one, with confidence intervals excluding
one. These differences also show that method rankings vary across
concepts and evaluation criteria.

Most notably, successful target suppression with preserved neighboring and
SciQ accuracy did not imply similarity to the concept-excluded twin. EMBER
had the highest $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ for Baseball while remaining the
most distant erasure method from their twins under KL and both signed-mean and
question-level NLL comparisons. On AI, RMU+EMBER achieved the highest
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ yet remained more than twice as far from the twin as the
full model under question-level NLL distance. SNMF showed the complementary disagreement:
its near-identity edits produced the lowest target KL and question-level
distance ratios below one, but only modest improvements over the full model
and little target erasure.
Efficacy--preservation and similarity to a
concept-excluded twin therefore capture distinct properties and should be
reported separately. This conclusion is limited to the tested models, three
concepts, fixed-answer evaluations, and one training seed. A useful next step
is to repeat the comparison across additional concepts and seeds and to test
whether erased models and concept-excluded twins differ in robustness to
relearning.
