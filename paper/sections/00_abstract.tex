Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress target knowledge while
preserving other behavior, but comparison with the original model does not
show whether erasure resembles training without the concept. We evaluate five
erasure conditions based on EMBER, RMU, and SNMF for Ancient Rome, Baseball, and
artificial intelligence against both a full model and matched concept-excluded
twins. Our primary metrics measure efficacy--preservation, absolute
question-level NLL proximity to the twin, and full-vocabulary KL proximity.
The methods that best balance suppression and preservation are generally not
those closest to the twin. SNMF often has the most favorable similarity ratios
but produces little target suppression, whereas EMBER-based edits with larger
target NLL changes typically move farther from the twin than the full model.
Thus, effective target suppression with preserved neighboring and
general-science performance does not necessarily reproduce the outcome of
concept exclusion.
