\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{When Evaluation Dimensions Disagree}

Baseball shows why one instrument cannot certify erasure. Token edits can
change short answer-option likelihoods while exclusion changes learning from
whole passages. Its small raw question contrast is largely reproduced by the
unrelated no-Rome twin; the chunk difference-in-differences has an internal
control for this run-level drift.

Accuracy moves only when the gold option crosses a distractor. EMBER's
accuracy-based specificity stayed at 1.0 through delta 200 for both concepts,
missing Rome's continuous collateral damage. Baseball's efficacy plateau let a
tie-break select delta 10. At Rome deltas 500 and 1,000 specificity finally
fell and the objective turned over, making 200 its full-grid argmax; the few
question differences near that peak do not establish a precise optimum.

\subsection{Concept-Dependent Erasure}

Baseball exclusion has 2.1 times Rome's text-NLL effect. A retrieval-based
audit found that the passages most relevant to Rome questions were more likely
to be blacklisted than those most relevant to Baseball questions; many Baseball
questions concern rules repeated across the corpus. This may explain part of
the instrument gap but is not a causal demonstration.

The selected Baseball feature has a higher concept-to-neutral activation ratio,
but matched strengths reveal a concept-by-delta interaction: effects are
similar at delta 10 and diverge at 200. Baseball chunks actually contain more
edited-token occurrences; Rome's damage rises more steeply with occurrence
count while Baseball's saturates. Whether distinct token types explain this
difference remains untested.

\subsection{Why Erasure Differs from Never-Training}

Exclusion changes an entire training trajectory; EMBER changes 72--76 input
rows after training in the two text-evaluated arms. Chunks without those tokens
cannot change under EMBER but still change under exclusion. Baseball likewise
shows observable forgetting with almost no alignment to the excluded parameter
displacement.

Parameter equality is not necessary for behavioral success, but directional
alignment tests the stronger claim that erasure ``undoes'' learning. EMBER does
not retrace the completed Baseball training displacement.

The AI result shows that EMBER can approach the excluded question profile.
The interval is wide, however, and text and parameter comparisons are missing.
On the same questions, SNMF moves in the wrong direction and strong RMU is
nonselective. Separate Rome SNMF text probes are near-null, but do not complete
the common-protocol cross-method comparison.
