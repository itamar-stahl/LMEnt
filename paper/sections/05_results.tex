\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Effect and Strength of Concept Exclusion}

Concept exclusion had its largest target effect for Rome: on-topic accuracy fell from
0.42 to 0.26 and correct-answer NLL increased by 1.016 nats per token. The
effects were smaller for Baseball (0.54 to 0.50; +0.377 NLL) and AI (0.54 to
0.48; +0.595 NLL). Rome also had the largest full--twin mean absolute NLL
distance and target KL: $D_{\mathrm{abs}}(F,T)=1.127$ and
$\mathrm{KL}(T\,\|\,F)=0.955$, compared with 0.817 and 0.707 for Baseball and
0.947 and 0.593 for AI. The Rome twin therefore provides the clearest target
contrast with the full model across these measures.

The similarity ratios are relative to metric-specific full--twin gaps. When
that gap is small, a ratio near one can result from an edit remaining close to
the full model rather than reproducing a substantial exclusion effect. Raw
full--twin differences, target suppression, and normalized proximity must
therefore be interpreted together. This qualification is particularly
important for SNMF on Baseball and AI, where favorable similarity rankings
coincide with limited target suppression.

The twins also differed from the full model on neighboring and SciQ questions,
and their $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ scores were 0.871 for Rome, 0.231 for Baseball,
and 0.343 for AI. A twin is therefore a reference for the specified exclusion
intervention, not an optimizer of efficacy--preservation. Complete comparisons
appear in Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.

\subsection{Erasure Efficacy and Preservation}

Table~\ref{tab:target} summarizes the three primary metrics. The leading erasure
method under $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ differed by concept: RMU for Rome (0.486),
EMBER for Baseball (0.639), and RMU+EMBER for AI (0.829), only 0.013 above
SNMF+EMBER. These results should not be treated as stable method rankings:
each accuracy changes by 0.02 per answer, and EMBER led RMU on Rome's
selection split (0.682 versus 0.219) before RMU led on the test split (0.486
versus 0.208).

The selected RMU+EMBER checkpoints remained weakly aligned with RMU's control
direction, indicating that the RMU representation-misdirection objective was
only weakly realized \citep{li2024wmdp}. Because selection optimized efficacy
and preservation after EMBER, we treat RMU+EMBER as a combined intervention
rather than attributing its results specifically to RMU.

\subsection{Twin Similarity}

The two primary twin-similarity metrics favored RMU or SNMF rather than the
EMBER-based conditions. For $R_{\mathrm{abs}}$, RMU was best for Rome (0.660),
while SNMF was best for Baseball (0.967) and AI (0.927). Only RMU on Rome and
SNMF on Rome and AI had $R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ intervals entirely below one. SNMF
also had the lowest $R_{\mathrm{KL}}$ for every concept (0.891--0.992), but
only its Rome interval, $[0.838,0.940]$, lay entirely below one. The Baseball
$[0.923,1.003]$ and AI $[0.954,1.030]$ intervals included one, providing no
clear separation from the full-model reference under this procedure. EMBER
and both ensembles exceeded one on both ratios for every concept, with
intervals entirely above one.

\subsection{Diagnostic Agreement and Cancellation}

The complementary diagnostics reveal differences that the primary ratios
alone do not. For AI, RMU had $R_{\mathrm{NLL}}=0.022$, indicating a near-zero
signed-mean discrepancy from the twin, but
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}=1.006$. Its positive and negative question-level
differences therefore largely canceled. For Baseball, SNMF was closer to the
twin on 66\% of questions, yet its $R_{\mathrm{abs}}=0.967$ confidence interval
included one. Its frequent question-level improvements therefore did not yield
a clear reduction in aggregate distance. Rome RMU gave the most consistent
evidence:
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}=0.660$ with an interval entirely below one, and it was closer
on 64\% of questions.

Signed-mean agreement, mean absolute distance, and the frequency of
question-level improvement thus capture different forms of similarity.
Agreement under one diagnostic alone is insufficient evidence that an edit
reproduces concept exclusion. Complete diagnostic results appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}.

\subsection{Cross-Concept Interpretation}

Figure~\ref{fig:efficacy-similarity} shows no consistent alignment between
efficacy--preservation and either measure of twin similarity. Rome RMU is the
clearest exception: it combines moderate $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ with lower
question-level distance from the twin. By contrast, the high-$H_{\mathrm{test}}$
Baseball and AI conditions lie above one on both similarity ratios, while the
SNMF points lie near or below one but have low $H_{\mathrm{test}}$.

This pattern reflects two distinct failure modes. A weak edit can remain close
to both the full model and the twin without reproducing the target effect of
concept exclusion. A stronger edit can suppress target behavior but move past
the twin, changing the model more than exclusion did at the evaluated
positions. Efficacy and twin similarity therefore provide complementary rather
than interchangeable evidence. Complete raw results appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.
