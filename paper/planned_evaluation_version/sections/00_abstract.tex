Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress knowledge of a target concept
while preserving model behavior elsewhere. Standard evaluations compare
erased and original models, but do not establish whether an erased model
resembles a matched model trained with direct learning signal from the target
concept excluded. We evaluate this question across three diverse
concepts---Ancient Rome, Baseball, and artificial intelligence---using a
matched concept-excluded reference for each. We compare five erasure
conditions based on EMBER, RMU, and SNMF with both the full model and its
matched reference. We measure target suppression, preservation on neighboring
and general questions, and similarity to the matched reference using
likelihood-ranked accuracy, correct-answer negative log-likelihood, and
teacher-forced full-vocabulary KL divergence. No method dominates across all
criteria. RMU most closely matches the twins' signed mean target-answer NLL,
but question-level absolute differences reveal cancellation for Baseball and
AI. SNMF yields the lowest target KL and the most consistent question-level
proximity to the twins. EMBER produces the strongest target-specific NLL
changes among the standalone methods and the highest combined
efficacy--preservation score for Baseball; RMU achieves the highest score for
Rome, and RMU+EMBER for AI. Both ensembles remain farther from the twins than
the full model under question-level target NLL distance.
Strong target suppression with preserved neighboring and SciQ accuracy does
not imply similarity to the concept-excluded twin: the highest-scoring method
is closest in mean target NLL for only one concept and never has the lowest
target KL. These results show that efficacy--preservation and similarity to a
matched exclusion reference capture distinct properties of concept erasure.
