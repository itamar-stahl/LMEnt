An erasure method can lower a model's score on a topic without making the model
resemble one that never received training signal from that topic. We test this
distinction using matched 1B-parameter models trained on an entity-annotated
Wikipedia corpus. For each of Ancient Rome, Baseball, and artificial
intelligence, one model receives no training loss on text segments linked to
the concept. We compare these models with EMBER, which edits selected input
embeddings, using questions, held-out text loss, parameter changes, and effects
on unrelated data. The selected Rome edit causes far more text loss than
concept exclusion, especially on passages containing many edited tokens;
Baseball's edit falls short of its text-loss reference. No tested Rome edit
strength matches both the mean and median exclusion effects. On AI questions,
EMBER comes close to the excluded model, although this does not establish
equivalence on text or parameters. Two additional methods that edit MLP weights
do not show the same selective question pattern in the completed AI comparison.
The Baseball EMBER edit is almost orthogonal to the parameter change caused by
concept exclusion. A lower target score
alone therefore does not establish resemblance to a controlled training
baseline.
