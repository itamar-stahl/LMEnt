\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Effect of Concept Exclusion}

We first compared each concept-excluded twin with the full model to establish
the behavioral effect of excluding the selected training chunks. The largest
target effect occurred for Ancient Rome: target accuracy fell from 0.42 to
0.26, correct-answer NLL increased by 1.016 nats per token, and
$\operatorname{KL}(T\,\|\,F)$ was 0.955. The corresponding effects were
smaller for Baseball, where accuracy fell from 0.54 to 0.50 and NLL increased
by 0.377, and for AI, where accuracy fell from 0.54 to 0.48 and NLL increased
by 0.595. Thus, the measured effect of concept exclusion varied substantially
across concepts.

The twins also differed from the full model outside the target questions. On
neighboring questions, the twin--full NLL differences were 0.171 for Rome,
0.093 for Baseball, and $-0.139$ for AI. On SciQ, they were 0.046,
$-0.093$, and 0.145, respectively. Full-vocabulary KL was nonzero on both
preservation groups, including cases in which option accuracy did not change.
Concept exclusion therefore affected more than the measured target answers.
These off-target changes are part of the twin outcome, but they are distinct
from preservation relative to the full model.

The twins' efficacy--preservation scores also differed across concepts:
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ was 0.871 for Rome, 0.231 for Baseball, and 0.343 for AI.
The lower Baseball and AI scores primarily reflect the twins' modest reductions
in target accuracy. This illustrates that the twin is a reference for the
outcome of concept exclusion, not necessarily an optimizer of the
efficacy--preservation score. Complete twin--full results appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.

\subsection{Erasure Efficacy and Preservation}

Table~\ref{tab:target} summarizes held-out target results for the five erasure
conditions and their concept-excluded references. Method rankings under
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ differed across concepts. For Rome, RMU obtained the
highest score among the erasure methods ($0.486$), followed by EMBER
($0.208$). RMU reduced target accuracy from 0.42 to 0.36, placing it between
the full model and the twin, but also reduced neighboring accuracy from 0.64
to 0.50. This loss of neighboring performance limited its combined score.
SNMF received a score of zero because its target accuracy, 0.48, exceeded the
full model's 0.42.

EMBER obtained the highest $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ for Baseball ($0.639$).
Its target accuracy fell from 0.54 to 0.40, while neighboring accuracy changed
from 0.44 to 0.42 and SciQ accuracy remained 0.52. RMU produced a smaller
target reduction, to 0.50, and SNMF remained close to the full model at 0.52.

For AI, RMU+EMBER obtained the highest score ($0.829$), narrowly exceeding
SNMF+EMBER ($0.816$). Standalone EMBER reduced target accuracy from 0.54 to
0.36 while changing neighboring accuracy from 0.50 to 0.48 and leaving SciQ
accuracy at 0.52. RMU+EMBER reduced target accuracy further to 0.32, while
SNMF+EMBER reached 0.34. Both ensembles received zero for Rome because neither
reduced target accuracy below the full model.

These are held-out outcomes rather than stable method orderings. Each accuracy
estimate is based on 50 questions and therefore changes by 0.02 when one answer
changes. For Rome, EMBER led RMU on the selection split
($H_{\mathrm{selection}}=0.682$ versus 0.219), whereas RMU led EMBER on the
test split (0.486 versus 0.208). On AI, the two ensemble scores differed by
only 0.013. These close and split-dependent rankings should therefore not be
interpreted as general method orderings.

All three selected RMU+EMBER checkpoints failed RMU's predeclared activity
check: their final cosine alignment with the control direction ranged from
0.076 to 0.285, below the 0.30 threshold. Because EMBER already supplied
target efficacy, the selection score favored weak RMU updates. These rows
describe the outcome of the frozen selection procedure, but they do not
provide evidence of a substantial additional RMU contribution.

\subsection{Signed Mean NLL and Distributional Similarity}

The twin-similarity metrics produced different rankings from
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$. RMU had the smallest absolute signed mean target NLL
difference from the twin for every concept. Its
$\Delta\mathrm{NLL}_{M-T}$ values were $-0.303$ for Rome, 0.247 for
Baseball, and $-0.013$ for AI. In particular, RMU nearly matched the twin's
mean target NLL for AI.

After normalization by the full--twin discrepancy, RMU's
$R_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ values were 0.298 for Rome, 0.655 for Baseball, and 0.022
for AI. These results indicate close agreement in the group mean, but they do
not establish agreement on individual questions because positive and negative
item-level differences can cancel.

SNMF had the lowest target KL among the erasure conditions for every concept:
0.851 for Rome, 0.680 for Baseball, and 0.588 for AI. However, it also changed
the full model least. Its target NLL increases from the full model were only
0.108, 0.033, and 0.081, respectively. Correspondingly, its
$R_{\mathrm{KL}}$ values were 0.891, 0.962, and 0.992. SNMF therefore reduced
the full model's divergence from the twin only modestly. Its low KL primarily
reflects a near-identity edit rather than strong target suppression.

EMBER showed the opposite pattern among the standalone methods. It produced
the largest target NLL increase from the full model for Rome, Baseball, and AI:
2.402, 1.294, and 2.220 nats per token. In every case, however, it moved beyond
the twin in the same direction, yielding twin-relative differences of 1.387,
0.918, and 1.625. Its target KL was also higher than that of the other
standalone methods.

The ensemble variants behaved similarly to EMBER. Their target NLL increases
ranged from 1.179 to 2.364, and every signed difference from the twin was
positive. Their KL values also exceeded the corresponding twin--full
baselines for every concept. Thus, EMBER and its ensembles strongly reduced
support for the target answers, but did not most closely reproduce the
distributions of the concept-excluded twins.

\subsection{Question-Level NLL Proximity}

The mean absolute per-question analysis qualified the signed-mean NLL
comparison. RMU was clearly closer to the twin than the full model for Rome,
with $R_{\mathrm{abs}}=0.660$ and a 95\% confidence interval of
$[0.513,0.827]$. For Baseball and AI, however, its intervals included one:
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}=1.227$ $[0.877,1.680]$ and
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}=1.006$ $[0.754,1.344]$, respectively.

The difference from the signed-mean results was substantial. For Baseball,
RMU's signed mean difference from the twin was only 0.247, but its mean
absolute question-level distance was 1.003. For AI, the corresponding values
were $-0.013$ and 0.953. RMU therefore matched the twin's average target NLL
closely because positive and negative question-level differences canceled,
especially for AI.

SNMF had $R_{\mathrm{abs}}<1$ for all three concepts and was closer than the
full model on 64--66\% of target questions. Its confidence intervals excluded
one for Rome and AI but included one for Baseball. This provides evidence of
small question-level improvements for Rome and AI, although these improvements
occurred alongside little target suppression.

EMBER was farther from the twin on average for every concept, with
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ values of 1.613 for Rome, 2.128 for Baseball, and 2.112
for AI. Both ensembles were also farther from the twin for every concept; all
six ensemble intervals excluded one, and the ensembles were closer than the
full model on only 28--44\% of questions. Complete estimates and confidence
intervals appear in Table~\ref{tab:question-level-similarity}.

\subsection{Relationship Between Efficacy and Twin Similarity}

The method with the highest $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ was closest to the twin in
signed mean target NLL only for Rome and never had the lowest target KL. For
Baseball, the highest-scoring method was EMBER, with
$R_{\mathrm{NLL}}=2.435$ and $R_{\mathrm{KL}}=2.082$. For AI, the
highest-scoring method was RMU+EMBER, with corresponding ratios of 2.807 and
3.420. Both ensembles exceeded one on both ratios for every concept.

The disagreement reflects the metrics' different references.
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ rewards target degradation and preservation relative to
the full model, whereas the NLL and KL ratios measure proximity to the twin.
A method can therefore obtain a high efficacy--preservation score by strongly
suppressing target answers while moving past, rather than toward, the twin.
Conversely, a small edit can remain close to both the full model and the twin
without producing substantial erasure. Complete normalized comparisons appear
in Table~\ref{tab:metric-agreement}.

\subsection{Selectivity and Variation Across Concepts}

The post-hoc NLL contrast measured whether changes were concentrated on target
questions rather than the neighboring and SciQ groups. EMBER's contrasts were
1.932 nats per token for Rome, 1.242 for Baseball, and 2.124 for AI. The
corresponding values were 1.701, 1.126, and 2.325 for RMU+EMBER, and 1.886,
1.286, and 2.145 for SNMF+EMBER. All ensemble confidence intervals excluded
zero.

RMU's contrasts were $-0.094$, 0.151, and 0.011, while SNMF's were 0.131,
0.088, and 0.121. EMBER and its ensembles therefore produced much larger
target-specific NLL changes than RMU or SNMF, even though they remained farther
from the twins. The contrast definition, component comparisons, and uncertainty
estimates appear in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}.

No method led under every criterion, and rankings varied across concepts. RMU
most closely matched the twins' signed mean target NLL but did not consistently
improve question-level proximity and also changed preservation groups. SNMF
made small edits that modestly improved some proximity measures but produced
little target suppression. EMBER and its ensembles produced the largest and
most target-specific NLL changes but generally moved beyond the twins.
Differences in corpus coverage and overlap with neighboring topics may
contribute to this variation, although the present measurements do not isolate
their effects.
