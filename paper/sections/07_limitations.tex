\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Limited compute restricted the study to one model family and size, one corpus,
one seed per condition, and three purposively chosen concepts. We evaluated a
1B OLMo-2 base model trained on LMEnt's Wikipedia corpus, whereas EMBER was
evaluated on the instruction-tuned Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B models
\citep{suslik2026ember}. Model scale is therefore confounded with differences
in architecture, training data, and instruction tuning. These differences may
affect the amount of target knowledge present before editing, the separability
of concept-related representations, and the response to each erasure method.
In particular, the modest baseline target accuracy in our setting leaves less
room to measure suppression and can make checkpoint comparisons sensitive to
individual questions. Our results should therefore be interpreted as an evaluation in a small
base-model setting, rather than a direct replication or contradiction of
the results reported by \citet{suslik2026ember} on larger instruction-tuned
models.

The full model and each twin used the same training configuration, but not
always the same accelerator. The full model and Rome twin trained entirely on
H200 GPUs; the Baseball twin also used H100s, and the AI twin included roughly
12 hours on an A6000. We therefore cannot rule out a minor hardware contribution
to the numerical differences for Baseball and AI.

The concept-excluded twins are approximate references. The selected QIDs may
miss indirect descriptions and may also include text about adjacent topics.
The loss mask therefore does not remove every possible source of target
knowledge and may affect related knowledge.

The evaluation uses one supplied correct continuation and three distractors
per question. It does not measure generated answers, and likelihood-ranked
accuracy depends on the supplied alternatives. Teacher-forced KL covers the
full next-token distribution along the correct continuation, but does not
follow predictions after an alternative token. SciQ measures only one aspect
of general preservation. Due to a preprocessing error, its items remained in
their original question format while target and neighboring items were
converted to declarative stems. We discovered the mismatch too close to the
submission deadline to rerun selection and evaluation. We expect limited
qualitative impact: full-model SciQ accuracy was 0.52 and remained between
0.50 and 0.52 across the selected erasure models, while
$R_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{KL}}$ use only target items. The mismatch
may nevertheless affect the exact checkpoint selections and
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ values. In addition, each group contains only 50 questions, so accuracy
changes in steps of 0.02 and the reported uncertainty does not include
variation across training seeds.

Checkpoint selection also has two specific limitations. No Rome or Baseball
SNMF candidate reduced target selection accuracy, so tie-breaking rather than
measured efficacy selected those checkpoints. Finally, selection optimized likelihood-ranked accuracy rather than distance
from the twin, so the selected checkpoints need not minimize NLL or KL. This
was also a deliberate design choice: our research question asks whether edits
selected for target suppression and preservation relative to the full model
also resemble the concept-excluded twin.

We did not test relearning or other knowledge-recovery attacks. Our conclusions
apply to the selected configuration; comparing how quickly erased models and
twins recover target behavior is a useful extension.
