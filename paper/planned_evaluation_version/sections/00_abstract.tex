Post-training concept erasure seeks to suppress target knowledge while
preserving model behavior elsewhere. Comparisons with the original model
measure these properties, but do not establish whether an erased model
resembles one trained without direct loss on concept-associated data. We test
this question for Ancient Rome, Baseball, and artificial intelligence using a
matched concept-excluded model for each concept. We compare five erasure
conditions based on EMBER, RMU, and SNMF with both the full model and its twin.
Our metrics are likelihood-ranked option accuracy, correct-answer negative
log-likelihood (NLL), and teacher-forced full-vocabulary KL on target,
neighboring, and SciQ questions. RMU is closest to the twins in signed mean
target NLL, but question-level differences reveal cancellation for Baseball
and AI. SNMF changes the full model least and has the lowest target KL, but
provides little target suppression. EMBER produces the largest target-specific
NLL changes among standalone methods and the highest efficacy--preservation
score for Baseball; RMU leads for Rome. The selected RMU+EMBER condition leads
for AI, although its RMU component fails the activity check. Both ensembles
are farther from the twins than the full model under question-level target NLL
distance. Across concepts, the highest-scoring method is closest in signed
mean target NLL only once and never has the lowest target KL. Efficacy and
preservation therefore do not imply similarity to a matched exclusion
reference in these experiments.
