\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-design}

\subsection{Models and Matched Training}

We train decoder-only OLMo-2 1B base models with 18 layers, hidden size 2048,
and 16 attention heads on LMEnt's 3.6B-token Wikipedia corpus. All models train
for two epochs (54,832 optimizer steps) with global batch size 131,072 tokens,
AdamW, peak learning rate $4\times10^{-4}$, 2,000 warm-up steps, and cosine
decay to $4\times10^{-5}$. The initialization seed is 12536 and the data seed
is 0.

The shared control receives loss on the complete corpus. Each concept-excluded
arm starts from the same seeded initialization and uses the same optimizer,
schedule, batch indices, and number of steps. Exclusion is applied only after a
batch has been composed: a matched chunk keeps its batch position, but every
label in that row is replaced with the ignore index. It contributes no loss or
gradient unless the all-masked-batch safety guard retains one row. One control
is shared by the Ancient Rome, Baseball, and AI arms. Training logs confirm
that the configured blacklists were applied.

\subsection{Concepts and Data Removal}

LMEnt associates chunks with candidate Wikidata entities derived from
hyperlinks, entity linking, coreference, and coreference clusters. For each
concept, we audit a set of Wikidata entity identifiers (QIDs) and use LMEnt's
source-specific evidence thresholds to find the chunks linked to them. We call
this set of chunks the blacklist and mask their labels during training. Ancient
Rome uses 56 entities and covers 65,844 chunks (0.628\% of the corpus). The
final set replaced an initial one-entity blacklist
after a corpus audit showed that it captured only a small fraction of sampled
Rome-bearing passages. The expanded set was estimated at approximately 46\%
coverage and 62.5\% precision on that audit.

Baseball uses 43 entities covering 80,466 chunks (0.766\%): core baseball
institutions and the 30 Major League Baseball franchises. Teams were included
after a corpus-only audit: the core set achieved 94.0\% estimated precision and
43.1\% coverage, while adding teams raised estimated coverage to 56.5\% at
87.2\% precision. Adding a player set produced only 0.1 percentage points of
additional coverage while reducing precision, so it was rejected. The tracked
configuration records that this choice preceded the question-answering
evaluation.

The AI blacklist resolves 31 entities to 19,818 unique chunks. A sampled audit
estimated 68.0\% precision and 70.1\% coverage.
The completed AI twin reached the same 54,832-step endpoint after several
resumed training legs; its final window loaded the expected blacklist and
masked 6,300 chunk occurrences in training batches. The comparison with erasure
uses a common question evaluation; matched text-loss and weight-space
comparisons are unavailable.

These definitions deliberately favor an auditable intervention over an
unverifiable claim of complete removal. Unlinked Roman context and general
baseball rules may remain elsewhere in the corpus. We therefore treat the
excluded model as the target induced by this particular data operation.
Pornography experiments are outside the scope of the main study.

\subsection{Erasure Methods}

All post-hoc methods begin from the completed shared control checkpoint.

\paragraph{EMBER.}
We factorize the control's input-embedding matrix into 100 sparse features
(global sparsity 0.02; seed 44). Candidate features are ranked by the ratio
of their mean absolute activation on 300 concept sentences to that on 300
neutral sentences. A Gemma-4-12B-IT judge then retains features it labels
concept-specific with confidence at least 0.85. For Rome, the rule for choosing
among factorization settings prioritizes judge confidence, then activation
ratio; this rule was fixed before the grid was complete.
The Baseball factorization setting was likewise selected on feature quality
before erasure, but
its detailed selection note is stored only on the cluster. Neither choice used
the concept-excluded outputs. Ancient Rome selects feature 11 and edits 76
embedding rows; Baseball selects feature 10 and edits 72. In both cases the
untied output head and all other tensors remain unchanged.
For the reported Rome and Baseball runs, EMBER tests edit strengths
$\delta\in\{0.5,1,2,5,10,50,100,200\}$. Delta scales the feature
contribution subtracted from each selected embedding row. EMBER chooses a
delta using efficacy on 50
concept-training questions and accuracy-based specificity on 50 neighboring
domain training questions. The held-out halves are not used for delta
selection. This chooses 200 for Ancient Rome and 10 for Baseball. Subsequent
fixed-delta sensitivity runs extend Rome to 500 and 1,000 and cross both
concepts at deltas 10 and 200; these were not used to reselect the reported
models. For AI, the judge selected features 26, 85, and 89, editing 187
embedding rows at delta 5. The tracked summary records the chosen setting but
does not establish the full AI candidate grid or its selection chronology.

\paragraph{RMU.}
The LMEnt adaptation optimizes selected MLP \texttt{down\_proj} matrices using
concept sentences as the forget set and neutral sentences as the retain set.
For the AI comparison on the same question set and scoring procedure, the
completed high-steering run at layer 5 uses steering strength 846.6, batch
size 1, and all 150 scheduled steps, with retain-loss weights
$\alpha\in\{10,100\}$. Both pass checks that the forget representations
rotated substantially and that enough update steps ran. An earlier Rome
layer grid includes runs that stopped short of the planned steps; a
separate full-schedule Rome run passes those checks but has not been evaluated
against its excluded twin on the paper's common question protocol. The tracked
summaries do not give a complete selection chronology for the AI settings.

\paragraph{SNMF.}
The LMEnt adaptation factorizes MLP activations, automatically labels candidate
concept features, and edits corresponding \texttt{up\_proj} and
\texttt{down\_proj} parameters. On AI, the common-protocol checkpoint reuses 84
selected MLP features from a cached factorization. In SNMF,
$\delta=1$ removes the selected component. Rome and Baseball probes at that
strength find little effect on held-out
accuracy; Rome SNMF checkpoints also show negligible change on a separate
held-out text sample. Earlier runs with $\delta>2$ inverted and amplified the
selected component rather than removing it, and are excluded from the erasure
comparison. The AI checkpoint's full factorization settings and selection
history are not recoverable from the tracked summary alone.
