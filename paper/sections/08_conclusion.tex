\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Post-training concept erasure did not consistently reproduce the behavior of
matched concept-excluded models. Rome RMU provided the clearest alignment
between target suppression and question-level movement toward the twin. More
generally, methods with favorable twin-similarity ratios often made weak edits,
whereas EMBER-based conditions with larger target NLL changes tended to move
beyond the exclusion reference. Neither ensemble moved closer to the twin
under mean absolute target-NLL distance or normalized KL distance.

A high efficacy--preservation score therefore did not imply similarity to the
concept-excluded twin. These criteria measure different properties and should
be reported separately. This conclusion is limited to three concepts, one
model family, fixed-answer evaluation, and one seed. Further work should test
additional concepts and seeds and compare erased models with their twins under
relearning.
