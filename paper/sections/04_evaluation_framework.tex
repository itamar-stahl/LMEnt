\section{Measuring Similarity to Never-Training}
\label{sec:evaluation}

For each concept we compare the fully trained control $M_C$, its
concept-excluded counterpart $M_N$, and an erased model $M_E$ produced from
$M_C$. For any measurement $f$, we first compare $f(M_N)$ and $f(M_E)$ with
$f(M_C)$. Their difference, $f(M_E)-f(M_N)$, is the \emph{residual}: zero
means the two models match on that
measurement. We examine four kinds of measurement rather than combining them
into one score.

\subsection{Behavioral Similarity}

EMBER supplies 100 target questions and 100 neighboring-domain questions per
concept, each divided into equal selection and held-out halves. Ancient Rome's
neighboring set covers other historical civilizations; Baseball's covers other
sports. The AI comparison uses the same 50-item-per-split completion protocol,
with all methods rescored by one harness. Because these base models do not
reliably follow a generated-letter multiple-choice format, questions are
rewritten as declarative stems and each answer option is scored as a
continuation. In a separate 50-question, four-shot generation probe, the models
emitted answer letters more often but scored only 8--11 questions correctly.
This motivated the continuation-based instrument used here.

For answer option $a$ and question stem $x$, we compute pointwise mutual
information (PMI) per character:
\begin{equation}
s(a,x)=\frac{\log P(a\mid x)-\log P(a\mid \varnothing)}{|a|_{\mathrm{char}}}.
\end{equation}
Here $\varnothing$ is the null context used to score the option alone.
The subtraction reduces preference for intrinsically common option strings and
the denominator mitigates option-length bias. Our primary per-question value is
the gold option's $s(a,x)$; models are compared on paired questions. Argmax
accuracy is secondary because 50 binary outcomes per split are too coarse to
resolve many changes visible in likelihood. Behavioral residuals are reported
as $s_E-s_N$, so negative values mean that erasure suppresses the gold answer
more strongly than concept exclusion.

\subsection{Text-Likelihood Similarity}

For the primary Rome and Baseball EMBER comparisons, models within each arm
score the same 3,000 blacklisted chunks and 5,004 non-blacklisted chunks sampled to match the
variable-sequence-length bucket distribution. A separate Rome SNMF probe uses
16,915 held-out and 5,044 control chunks and must not be combined numerically
with the primary sample. For
model pair $(A,B)$, let $\Delta_H(A,B)$ be the mean paired increase in
per-token negative log-likelihood (NLL) on held-out concept chunks when moving
from $A$ to $B$ (that is, $\mathrm{NLL}_B-\mathrm{NLL}_A$), and let
$\Delta_R(A,B)$ be the corresponding increase on control chunks. We report the
difference-in-differences (DiD):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DiD}(A,B)=\Delta_H(A,B)-\Delta_R(A,B),
\end{equation}
which subtracts global model drift. We also inspect quantiles of the per-chunk
shift: equal means can conceal a uniform mild degradation versus a small tail
of severely damaged passages. For the erased-minus-excluded residual, positive
values mean that erasure produces higher loss than exclusion and negative
values mean that it does not reach the exclusion-induced loss increase.

This measures loss on observed text, not divergence between full output
distributions.

\subsection{Parameter-Space Similarity}

Flattening aligned floating-point parameters, define the erasure update
$d_E=W_E-W_C$ and target displacement $d_N=W_N-W_C$. We report
\begin{align}
\mathrm{cosine} &= \frac{d_E^\top d_N}{\lVert d_E\rVert\lVert d_N\rVert},\\
\mathrm{progress} &= \frac{d_E^\top d_N}{\lVert d_N\rVert^2},\\
\mathrm{residual} &= \frac{\lVert d_E-d_N\rVert}{\lVert d_N\rVert}.
\end{align}
Cosine measures direction, progress measures how much of the target direction
the edit traverses, and residual equals zero only at the excluded model. Global
measurements include all aligned parameters; embedding-only results isolate the
space EMBER can directly change. Hardware differences between runs are
discussed under Limitations.

\subsection{Specificity and Capability Preservation}

We assess specificity---preservation of unrelated performance---with
neighboring-domain questions, length-matched general corpus chunks, and the
general-science multiple-choice benchmark SciQ. A
desirable edit approaches $M_N$ on target measurements without moving farther
from it on controls. Neighboring questions are semantically demanding but
small; control-chunk NLL has much higher power but is not topic matched; general
benchmarks can detect broad capability loss but need not be concept-sensitive.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

Question and same-set chunk contrasts are paired by item. We report the mean
paired change divided by its standard deviation ($d_z$). Existing question
summaries include paired
$t$-tests and sign-flip permutation tests, while chunk summaries use sign-flip
tests. For the held-out-versus-control difference-in-differences, whose two
sets are unpaired, we permute set labels. Bootstrap intervals are available for
the AI question residuals, but not for every reported contrast. These
statistics quantify item-level uncertainty only; with one training seed per
condition they do not measure training-run variability. Erasure settings are
selected without consulting the final concept-excluded comparison wherever the
record permits; any exception must be labeled exploratory.
