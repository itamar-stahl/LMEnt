Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress target knowledge while
preserving other behavior, but comparison with the original model does not
show whether erasure resembles training without the concept. We evaluate five
erasure conditions based on EMBER, RMU, and SNMF for Ancient Rome, Baseball, and
artificial intelligence against both a full model and matched concept-excluded
twins. Our primary metrics measure efficacy--preservation, absolute
question-level NLL proximity to the twin, and full-distribution KL proximity.
No method performs best across these criteria. RMU is closest under absolute NLL proximity
for Rome, while SNMF is closest for Baseball and AI and has the lowest
normalized KL for all three concepts, but produces little target suppression.
EMBER produces the largest target-specific NLL changes among standalone
methods. Efficacy--preservation winners vary across concepts and usually
remain far from their twins. Thus, effective target suppression with preserved
neighboring and general-science performance does not necessarily reproduce
the outcome of concept exclusion.
