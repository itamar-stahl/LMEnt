\section{Checkpoint Selection}
\label{app:checkpoint-selection}

We selected checkpoints separately for each combination of concept and
erasure method. The candidate grid contained 64 previously trained models,
distributed as shown in Table~\ref{tab:selection-grid-size}. No candidate was
trained or modified during selection.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    Concept & EMBER & RMU & SNMF \\
    \midrule
    Ancient Rome & 12 & 8 & 4 \\
    Baseball & 9 & 8 & 3 \\
    AI & 9 & 8 & 3 \\
    \midrule
    Total & 30 & 24 & 10 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Number of candidate checkpoints evaluated for each method and
  concept.}
  \label{tab:selection-grid-size}
\end{table}

Selection used 50 target questions, 50 neighboring-topic questions, and the
same 50 SciQ questions for every concept. For each question, we scored all
four supplied options as continuations of the context. The score of option
$o$ was its summed token log-probability divided by its character length,
and the option with the largest score was treated as the model's prediction:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{o}_M(x)
=\arg\max_o
\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n_o}\log p_M(o_t\mid x,o_{<t})}
{|o|_{\mathrm{char}}}.
\end{equation}
Accuracy was the fraction of questions for which $\widehat{o}_M(x)$ was the
correct option. This likelihood-ranking procedure avoided requiring the base
model to generate an answer letter.

For each group $g$, we converted candidate accuracy to retention relative to
the full model $F$, correcting for the four-choice chance level:
\begin{equation}
r_g(M)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(
\frac{\operatorname{Acc}_g(M)-0.25}
{\operatorname{Acc}_g(F)-0.25}\right).
\end{equation}
The full model exceeded chance in every selection group. We defined target
erasure efficacy as $e(M)=1-r_{\mathrm{target}}(M)$ and preservation as
\begin{equation}
p(M)=\operatorname{HM}\!\left(
r_{\mathrm{neighbor}}(M),r_{\mathrm{SciQ}}(M)\right).
\end{equation}
The final selection score was
\begin{equation}
H_{\mathrm{selection}}(M)=\operatorname{HM}\!\left(e(M),p(M)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{HM}(a,b)=2ab/(a+b)$ when both arguments are positive
and zero otherwise. This construction requires a candidate to reduce target
accuracy while preserving both neighboring and SciQ accuracy; a low value on
either axis limits the harmonic mean.

We ranked candidates first by $H_{\mathrm{selection}}$, then by preservation,
and then by efficacy. Remaining exact ties were resolved by a fixed
method-specific order: smaller edit intensity for EMBER; smaller layer, band,
and $\alpha$ tuple for RMU; and a declared selection-mode and edited-side order
for SNMF. The tie-breaking rule was fixed without consulting the twins or any
test result. Table~\ref{tab:selected-checkpoints} records the resulting
configurations.

\begin{table*}[t]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{llll}
    \toprule
    Concept & EMBER & RMU & SNMF \\
    \midrule
    Ancient Rome & $\delta=200$ & layer 6, high band, $\alpha=10$
      & ratio selection, output side$^{\dagger}$ \\
    Baseball & $\delta=10$ & layer 6, high band, $\alpha=10$
      & ratio selection, both sides$^{\dagger}$ \\
    AI & $\delta=500$ & layer 6, high band, $\alpha=10$
      & ratio selection, input side \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Configurations selected using $H_{\mathrm{selection}}$.
  $^{\dagger}$All SNMF candidates for this concept had zero efficacy and hence
  $H_{\mathrm{selection}}=0$; the displayed configuration was determined by
  the fixed tie-breaking order rather than a measured efficacy difference.}
  \label{tab:selected-checkpoints}
\end{table*}

The concept-excluded twins and the held-out target, neighboring, and SciQ
questions were not loaded or inspected during selection. After the nine
configurations were frozen, we evaluated them on the test splits and computed
$H_{\mathrm{test}}$ with the same formula. Neither $H_{\mathrm{test}}$ nor the
subsequent NLL and KL comparisons with the twins were used to revise the
selected configurations.
