\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Concept Erasure and Machine Unlearning}

Machine unlearning broadly seeks to remove the influence of selected training
data without the cost of retraining from scratch. Concept erasure operates at a
less literal granularity: the target is a semantic topic that may be expressed
across many documents and represented diffusely throughout a network. It also
differs from factual editing, which usually replaces or inserts a small number
of associations, and from refusal tuning, which can suppress an answer while
leaving the underlying knowledge accessible.

Erasure evaluations commonly balance \emph{efficacy}, reduced performance on
target data, against \emph{specificity}, preservation of neighboring and
general capabilities. Robustness tests additionally attempt to recover the
target through prompting, relearning, or other attacks. These measurements are
necessary, but comparison only to the original model leaves the desired
endpoint undefined. Our work instead treats controlled concept exclusion during
pretraining as a reference state.

\subsection{EMBER: Embedding-Based Concept Erasure}

Many erasure methods intervene in transformer MLP blocks while leaving token
embeddings unchanged. EMBER is motivated by the observation that embeddings
can retain concept-bearing directions even after an internal-layer edit
\citep{suslik2026ember}. It factorizes the token-embedding matrix into sparse,
interpretable features; identifies features activated by concept sentences more
than neutral sentences; and uses an automatic judge to retain features judged
specific to the target. For a small set of tokens associated with each accepted
feature, EMBER subtracts a scaled feature contribution from their embedding
rows. The resulting module can be applied alone or after an MLP erasure method.
Its localization is attractive for specificity, but it also makes its update
structurally different from the distributed parameter change caused by training
without a subset of data.

\subsection{MLP-Based Erasure Methods}

Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU) updates selected MLP
parameters so that forget-set inputs approach a random target representation,
while a retain loss keeps benign activations close to those of a frozen model
\citep{li2024wmdp}. It therefore changes the mapping implemented by selected
layers rather than individual token embeddings.

SNMF decomposes MLP activations into sparse features expressed as combinations
of co-activated neurons \citep{shafran2026snmf}. Concept-associated features
can then be selected and removed through corresponding changes to MLP
projection matrices. In our study RMU and SNMF are comparison methods: they test
whether conclusions about similarity to never-training are specific to EMBER's
embedding intervention or recur for internal-layer editing.

\subsection{Never-Trained Models as an Evaluation Baseline}

LMEnt provides a Wikipedia-based pretraining corpus annotated with entity
mentions, an entity-retrieval index, matched language models, and intermediate
checkpoints \citep{gottesman2025lment}. These resources make causal
interventions on entity-associated training data feasible. Related suites
approach controlled knowledge acquisition from complementary directions.
Hubble inserts selected passages into otherwise matched pretraining runs
\citep{wei2025hubble}; Deep Ignorance filters safety-relevant topics before
pretraining \citep{obrien2025deepignorance}. Such controls support a stronger
question than whether erasure changes a model: whether it approaches the model
produced when the relevant learning signal is absent.

This reference is not absolute ground truth. A semantic concept can appear in
unlinked or indirect text, and a model can infer it from related evidence.
Consequently, our claim is comparative: we measure proximity to a specific,
auditable data-exclusion intervention, not proof that either model contains no
trace of the concept.
