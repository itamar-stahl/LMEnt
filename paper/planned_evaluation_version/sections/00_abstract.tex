Post-training concept erasure aims to suppress target knowledge while
preserving other behavior, but comparison with the original model does not
show whether erasure resembles training without the concept. We evaluate five
conditions based on EMBER, RMU, and SNMF for Ancient Rome, Baseball, and
artificial intelligence against both a full model and matched concept-excluded
twins. Using likelihood-ranked accuracy, correct-answer NLL, and
teacher-forced full-vocabulary KL on target, neighboring, and SciQ questions,
we find no uniformly best method. RMU is closest to the twins in signed mean
target NLL, but mean absolute per-question NLL distance shows it is clearly
closer than the full model only for Rome. SNMF changes the full model least and has the
lowest target KL, but weakly suppresses target knowledge. EMBER produces the
largest target-specific NLL changes among standalone methods.
Efficacy--preservation winners vary across concepts and usually remain far
from their twins. Thus, effective target suppression with preserved
neighboring and general-science performance does not necessarily reproduce
concept-excluded training.
